THE THREAD THROUGH MY WORK

The tool changes.
The listening stays.

A good question can change
the whole story.

My route into AI runs through documentary storytelling, communication, learning design, and UX research. Each taught me to ask who the work is for, what they are trying to do, and what would help them move forward.

As an AI Analyst at Arizona State University, I worked on conversational experiences and proposed learner-support workflows. In my independent tools and teaching, I keep coming back to the same practical question: what does the person need to judge? A prompt revision can lose a requirement. A source bundle can be too narrow. A more vivid draft can add a claim nobody supplied.

I like making those moments visible enough to discuss and act on. Outside product work, I run tabletop games: another setting where a useful structure still depends on listening, adapting, and paying attention to the people in it.

Understand the actual work

Learn the task, constraints, sources and review decisions with the people doing it. Choose a useful starting point before building an AI workflow.

02

Make failure inspectable

Keep output requirements and source references visible. A shorter prompt or a fluent answer still needs to satisfy the original task.

03

Teach the decisions

Use concrete demonstrations and guided practice. Help people explain a revision and recognize when the result needs human review.
