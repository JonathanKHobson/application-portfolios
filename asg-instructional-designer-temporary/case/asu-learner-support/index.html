THE PROBLEM

What needed to become clearer?

A learner-support system needs more than personalized wording. It needs a way to check whether the context and suggested next task make sense for the person.

MY CONTRIBUTION

Turn the intent into an activity people can use.

As an AI Analyst at ASU Learning Innovations in 2024, I proposed a staged workflow for personalized learner tasks. A mentor would validate the learner context, then review or revise generated tasks before they reached the learner. The proposal made those review points part of the process.

INSIDE THE WORK

01 Check the learner context before generating the next task.02 Give the mentor a decision, not just a finished answer.Outcome & reflection

DECISION 01

Check the learner context before generating the next task.

My proposal condensed learner information, then gave the mentor a point to validate or revise that context. The check was intended to catch a misinterpretation before it became the basis for later suggestions.

DECISION 02

Give the mentor a decision, not just a finished answer.

A second review point sat after task generation and refinement. The mentor could confirm or modify the output before tasks moved toward the learner. That made responsibility visible in the proposed workflow.

OUTCOME & REFLECTION

A proposal with review built into the sequence.

The original proposal and flowchart make two mentor checkpoints explicit: validate the learner context, then review the generated tasks. They establish my workflow-design contribution. Deployment, learner results, and time savings are not established by these artifacts.

The participation journey starts before the activity.

My workshop work reinforced the same concern in a different form. Before an April 2024 AI event, I asked attendees what they wanted from the session. An ASU-only Zoom setting nevertheless blocked external attendees. I acknowledged the problem and offered remedies. That experience makes access setup part of how I think about learning design: an activity cannot help someone who cannot enter it.

The next question I would test

For a future session, test the actual invitation and tool access as an external attendee, and make an alternative participation route visible.
