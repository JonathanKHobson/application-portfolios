Put the mentor’s judgment into the workflow.

As an AI Analyst at ASU Learning Innovations in 2024, I proposed a staged workflow for personalized learner tasks. A mentor would validate the learner context, then review or revise generated tasks before they reached the learner. The proposal made those review points part of the process.

At ASU Knowledge Enterprise, I developed digital learning materials and workshops with accessibility improvements. For Coursera, I created original demonstrations and revised quiz content for a beginner ChatGPT project. That teaching work gives me concrete tasks to draw on when reviewing an AI-generated explanation or practice activity.

I also built and released Prompt Compass, a local tool for diagnosing, improving, comparing and reusing prompts. One designed regression example compares a specific JSON instruction with a shorter rewrite. The rewrite loses required fields and a human review condition, making the failure visible enough to explain.

For a Meridial training scenario, I would start with the learning goal and assigned review criteria, check factual claims against approved sources, and record the input, output, missing condition and reasoned correction. I would distinguish a subject-matter error from unclear instructions or an assessment that does not test its goal. This is a proposed approach.
