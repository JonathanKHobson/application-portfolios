THE THREAD THROUGH MY WORK

Before I studied products,
I studied stories.

A good question can change
the whole story.

Documentary filmmaking taught me to stay curious when someone’s experience did not fit the story I expected. That habit still guides my research: listen carefully, look for the exception, and be willing to change the question.

My background spans communication, learning design, and UX research. In the Oak work, those interests meet in practical questions: does a requested feature address the barrier people described? Does an explanation of privacy answer a concern about control? What evidence would change the team’s next step? I like making those questions specific enough to investigate together.

I work across product research and design, and volunteer as a UX Research Lead at Oppia. Away from work, I run tabletop games. Facilitation, pacing, and leaving room for quieter voices matter there too. It is another place where listening changes what happens next.

01

Understand a learner’s next step

Study what someone needs to accomplish and where a digital experience becomes unclear. Connect interviews, observation or concept testing to a specific decision the team needs to make.

02

Combine the relevant signals

Bring survey findings and behavioral signals alongside qualitative observations. Check what each source can support before using it to explain a pattern or choose a direction.

03

Build a shared research practice

Make study plans, source records and interpretations available for discussion. Work with designers and engineers so findings can guide an iteration and help collaborators develop their research judgment.

ASU LEARNING INNOVATIONS · 2024 PROPOSAL

Keep human judgment in the workflow.

At ASU, I proposed a staged prompt workflow for personalized learner tasks. A mentor would validate learner context, then confirm or revise generated tasks before learners received them.

The proposal shows how I approached learner context and review of generated advice. The diagram reconstructs the documented workflow, with both mentor review points visible. Deployment and measured learning outcomes are not established.
