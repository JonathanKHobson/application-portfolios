THE THREAD THROUGH MY WORK

I started by listening
to people’s stories.

A good question can change
the whole story.

I came to UX through documentary filmmaking. Listening closely, asking a better question, and deciding what belongs in the story were part of that work. I still use those habits when I look at a product: what is the person trying to understand, and what detail will help them move forward?

My background also includes technical communication, instructional design, and UX research. In the work shown here, those interests meet in small decisions: whether a saved question keeps its context, whether a comparison explains its reference point, and whether a page title confirms where someone has arrived. I like making those details clear enough for a team to examine together.

Away from product work, I run tabletop games. Facilitation, pacing, and adapting to a group's choices are part of that craft. It gives me another place to work with the same ingredients I care about in design: people, structure, and enough room for something unexpected to happen.

01

Clarify the task

Use research and close review to understand the audience, the intended action and the constraints. Turn an unclear request into a flow or mockup that the team can discuss.

02

Give visual choices a purpose

Use hierarchy, labels and color roles to make an interface understandable. Explain the decision and compare alternatives with the people responsible for the work.

03

Work through the details

Take stakeholder feedback into the next iteration. Document behavior and implementation questions, then check the design against the agreed task and scope.
