Preparation you can see.

These are materials from my own campaigns and adventures. Each serves a different need at the table: keeping continuity, giving a character a motive, or making a mission understandable.

01 · Campaign continuity

Peasants to Kings

A persistent homebrew world needs a shared memory. I developed linked character arcs, faction records, maps, and session recaps so the story has a reference point as it grows.

The map puts the geography in reach. The accompanying lore and recaps connect places to the choices made there.

My contribution
Worldbuilding, campaign preparation, reference materials, and ongoing continuity.

02 · One-shot preparation

Peril to Profit

A Daggerheart adventure designed for six players and three to four hours. I prepared pregenerated characters, handouts, maps, and pacing guidance around that scope.

Character biographies and fictional debt cards give each player a personal reason to engage with the premise.

Remote adaptation
The original uses a physical tower mechanic. An online version would need an agreed replacement before I offered the session remotely.

At the online table

Start with the people
playing the game.

My public GM profile centers on player choices, plain rules explanations, and sharing the spotlight. I use Discord, Owlbear Rodeo, and Zoom for online play.

For a Bigsby session, I would agree on tone and boundaries first, explain the first few choices, and make it easy to pause or ask a question. A short recap gives returning players a shared starting point.

This is my proposed approach for Bigsby. Session length, preparation, and scheduling would be agreed together.
